The line's mark comes in three levels, chosen by the size it is drawn at: L,
the plated mark with its metaphor, from 64px up; M, the plated mark with its
code alone, from 28 to 63; S, the filled tile, at 27 and below, where nothing
finer survives. Every product of the line has put the wrong level somewhere:
a whole .ico cut from the S tile, so the desktop showed a coloured blob; the
S tile in a site's header; a site's copy of the mark a version behind its
master. Each was found by someone looking at a screen, months later.

The rule reads the files instead. The masters are assets/logo.svg (L),
assets/logo-m.svg (M) and assets/logo-s.svg (S). A site's header
(src/assets/logo.svg) must be the L master byte for byte, a favicon.svg the S
master, and every apple-touch-icon.png the one in assets/. Each image inside
an .ico is read against the levels the masters draw at its size, which tells
all three apart; one that is close to none of them was not drawn from them, and
is held only by its share of plate, which the filled S tile hardly has. No fixed
pixel would do: where a level's code and metaphor fall is the product's. The
images must be listed largest first: Windows picks by size, but Tauri takes the
first one as the window's icon, and a title bar stretched out of 16 pixels is
how kilna shipped once.

`lyrn assets` draws every one of these from the masters, by the rule, and
`lyrn doctor --fix` copies the masters over stale copies, reorders an .ico and
draws a wrong level again with the same code. An exporter of the repository's
own - docs/export-assets.mjs, as the line once wrote them - is a second
drawing of the rule and warns: nine products of fifteen got theirs wrong.
