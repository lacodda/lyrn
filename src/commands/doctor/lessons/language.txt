Every product of the line speaks English first - its README, its docs, its
comments, its messages - and a translation lives where translations go: a
directory or a file named for its language, as docs/ru/, locales/ru.json or
README.ru.md, or a directory of translations, as i18n/ or locales/.

Text in another script is not by itself a finding. A product that handles
Russian has Russian in its tests and in the examples its docs show, and that
is data. What the rule looks for is prose: four words or more in a row in
another script, outside the places its translations go. In a test's material
- tests/, fixtures/, a *.test.ts, a Rust file below #[cfg(test)] - only the
comments are read.

It also looks for words typed partly in one keyboard layout and partly in the
other. "command" whose first letter is the Cyrillic one reads as a word and
is not one: search misses it and the spell checker skips it. lyrn's own changelog carried one for
three years.

Commit subjects in another language only warn: the changelog is made of them,
and rewriting a shared history costs every clone.
