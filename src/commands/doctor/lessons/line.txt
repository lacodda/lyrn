Everything that draws the line as a whole reads one registry: the list of
products `rigger export --line` publishes from the record, with each one's
mark, colour, shape and the address of its documentation. dowel's product
switcher and accents, the tiles on a README, a page listing the line - all of
them take a product's colour and its address from there, while the product's
own buttons take theirs from lyrn.toml and its site is built for whatever
astro.config.mjs says.

When the two disagree, neither file looks wrong on its own: the switcher shows
the product in one colour and the product opens in another, or sends a reader
to an address its site is not built for. So the doctor reads the registry once
and holds each repository to its entry: the colour against [project] accent in
lyrn.toml, and the address against the site's `site` and `base`. The shape the
registry gives a product is not held to its form: the form is how the
repository is built, the shape what the product is to whoever uses it - a
service with a web UI is a web product.

Only what both sides say is compared. A product recorded before its mark was
drawn is not wrong for having no colour yet, and a machine without rigger has
no registry to hold anything to: the rule is skipped and says why. Put right
whichever side is wrong - lyrn.toml and the site in the repository, or the
record with `rigger project mark <name> --accent` and `--docs`.
