`rust-version` in Cargo.toml is a promise to everyone who runs `cargo install`:
Cargo believes it and picks dependency versions by it. Nothing checks it on its
own. The first time the code uses a newer API, the promise is broken and the
crate fails to build for exactly the people it claims to support.

So the standard pairs the number with a CI job named `msrv` that reads it from
the manifest with `cargo metadata` and builds on that toolchain. Reading it,
rather than writing the number into the workflow too, matters: two copies agree
the day they are written and disagree after the next bump.

The rule fails a Rust manifest (Cargo.toml, src-tauri/Cargo.toml) with no
rust-version, a declared version no job builds on, and a job that builds on a
different number or on a channel such as `stable`. It warns when the job
repeats the number by hand. The job `lyrn new` writes is the model.

`lyrn msrv` finds the number: it starts at the floor the edition and the
dependencies set, builds on each toolchain as the job does, and writes the
oldest that passes.
