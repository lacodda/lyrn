A public history is read under a real name. An old alias, a misspelt surname, a
machine that once committed as user@localhost - each appears on every page of
the project as somebody else.

The lesson: one repository's history carried a surname spelt two ways and a
second identity under an old handle. Pushed commits are not rewritten, so the
fix was `.mailmap`, git's own file for saying who is who - and the rule is the
gate that keeps it true.

Every author in the history, and every person named in a `Co-authored-by`
trailer, is resolved through .mailmap and has to come out as either the global
git identity of this machine or someone .mailmap names as themselves. An alias
of yours is mapped to you:

    Your Name <you@example.com> old-handle <old@example.com>

and anyone else who wrote a commit is named:

    Their Name <them@example.com>

An assistant named as co-author is left to `ai-traces`: no .mailmap line should
make it somebody. On CI, where a runner has no identity of its own, .mailmap is
the whole list.
