The standard turns off Dependabot's pull requests and keeps its other half as
an audit workflow: advisories and licenses checked on every push and every
Monday. The trade only holds if the audit sees everything Dependabot would.

It did not. The audit read the lockfile at the top of the repository, and the
documentation site under docs/ had a lockfile of its own that no job read.
Twenty-two advisories accumulated there, eight of them high, and the first
anyone heard of them was an email from GitHub.

The rule lists every tracked Cargo.lock, pnpm-lock.yaml and package-lock.json
and fails each one no workflow audits: `cargo deny check` or cargo-deny-action
(with the advisories check), `cargo audit`, `pnpm audit` or `npm audit` run in
its directory - by `working-directory`, a job's defaults, `cd`, `--dir` or
`--manifest-path`. Lockfiles under fixtures/ and testdata/ are left out.

A documentation site added with `lyrn new <name> --form docs` brings its own
docs-audit.yml.
